How we’re choosing

Two constraints do most of the work

  • Patricia’s journey is the constraint, not the average journey. She’s flying out of Guadalajara, and the party is for her, so a destination that’s wonderful for five of us and awkward for her is simply the wrong destination. Everyone else can absorb a connection. We’d rather she didn’t have to.
  • Inside Mexico she flies domestic. No immigration hall, no customs, no passport queue. That is worth more than any single feature of any single resort, and nowhere outside Mexico can match it.
  • Not a sealed resort bubble. Patricia asked for a resort; we also want real places within day-trip reach rather than a week behind one gate. That is really Karen’s Cancún idea generalised, and it rules out a few otherwise fine islands.

The six options

Ranked. Every one of them was checked properly, including the two we ended up saying no to.

01Riviera Maya / CancúnRecommended

Karen’s suggestion, and it holds up better than anything else we looked at. This is the only candidate where all six origin cities fly nonstop. Patricia is 2h40 from Guadalajara with about 55 departures a week to choose from, and because it is a domestic flight she skips immigration entirely. Karen has a KLM nonstop from Amsterdam on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Saturdays. Ben has three carriers out of Boston, Christy has a wide choice from O’Hare, and Copa serves Panama year-round. With 15 people converging from three continents, connection-free travel is worth more than any single feature of any single resort.

The landmark half of the brief is the region’s whole identity. Tulum’s clifftop Maya site is about an hour from Playa del Carmen and is the easy, photogenic outing. Chichén Itzá is 2.5 hours each way, so it is a full day and a hot one, and it shouldn’t sit next to a travel day. Cobá is quieter at 1.5 hours, and dozens of cenotes sit inside an hour, which makes them the best low-effort excursion for a mixed-age group. On lodging there are two shapes: Hotel Xcaret México bundles the Chichén Itzá tour, the cenote tour and the parks with transport into the room rate, which removes the hardest part of moving a group this size, at roughly $600–1,500 a room per night; or a mid-range block at Bahía Príncipe, Iberostar or Barceló at $280–500. One thing to plan around: the clear-water window runs November through April, so we sit at the good end of it, but sargassum can start arriving in April in a bad year. A north- or west-facing beach hedges that.

02PanamaStrong runner-up

Copa’s hub does something no other option manages: it reaches five of our six origins nonstop, and the sixth party already lives there. Guadalajara to Tocumen runs daily at 4h08, KLM flies Amsterdam, and Copa serves Boston, Chicago and Denver directly. The resort would be The Buenaventura Golf & Beach Resort at Playa Blanca, about 80 minutes west of Tocumen with a hotel shuttle, or 20 minutes from the Río Hato regional strip. The landmarks are genuinely close and genuinely good, which is the part people underrate: Casco Viejo, the Miraflores Locks and the Canal itself, the crater town of El Valle de Antón, and the Gamboa rainforest.

Three things keep it out of first place. April is Panama’s hottest month, with average highs above 33°C and the dry season ending mid-month, and sustained heat like that shapes how much of the day is usable, rather than being a footnote. The Pacific beaches at Playa Blanca are grey-sand and strongly tidal, which is not the postcard anyone pictures when they hear the word resort. And least measurable but most real: it is home turf. Tom and Juan are in Panama constantly and Pancho and Gloria live there, so a milestone birthday risks feeling like a normal visit with extra relatives. That last one is taste rather than logistics, and if the group disagrees, Panama is very defensible on the numbers alone.

03Puerto VallartaFallback

By a wide margin this is the kindest destination for Patricia. It is a one-hour flight from Guadalajara, and if flying turns out to be the problem rather than the distance, it is the only place on this list she could reach by car, in about three and a half hours. The landmarks are better than the region’s package-holiday reputation suggests: the Malecón and the Zona Romántica in town, the restored mountain mining town of San Sebastián del Oeste, boat-only Yelapa, Sayulita up the coast, and the Marietas islands. Chicago, Denver and Los Angeles all fly nonstop, so half the group is well served.

It breaks on the other half. There is no nonstop from Boston at all, which puts Ben’s party on a connection each way, and Europe needs two connections. Copa flies Panama to Puerto Vallarta only twice a week, which means Pancho and Gloria’s travel days stop being a choice and start being whatever the airline offers. We are keeping this one in reserve rather than discarding it. If it turns out Patricia would rather not fly far, Puerto Vallarta is the answer, and the rest of us absorbing one connection each is a fair price for the guest of honour having the easiest journey of anyone.

04ArubaNo

This was on Juan’s own list, and an early draft of our notes rejected it for the wrong reason. We had written that Aruba was two stops from Guadalajara. That was wrong: Copa connects it in one stop on a single ticket, Guadalajara to Panama to Aruba, with both legs running daily at about 4h08 and 1h57. Correcting that mattered, so here is the actual objection.

It is the shape of the day, and it is a pain for everyone rather than only for Patricia. Copa leaves Guadalajara between 00:11 and 12:22 and leaves Panama for Aruba between 09:12 and 19:01, so the two realistic pairings are a midnight departure with a dawn layover, or a midday departure landing in Aruba near 10pm. Either is a 13-hour travel day bracketed by a bad hour. Meanwhile the rest of us are fighting Aruba’s season: its peak runs mid-December to mid-April, so April 7–14 sits exactly where the seasonal North American service winds down. American from Chicago and Delta from Boston are both seasonal, and Denver currently runs Wednesday to Saturday only. The one party Aruba suits perfectly is Karen’s, with a daily KLM nonstop at 10h45. So it is best for the shortest journey and worst for the person the week exists for. Add the thinnest landmark list of the shortlist, since Arikok park, Oranjestad and the natural pool do not carry seven days, and it comes off the list on the merits.

05Lima and Paracas, PeruRight idea, wrong trip

This was Patricia’s own suggestion, and checking it properly turned up two things in its favour that we had assumed went the other way. We thought April in Lima was grey and that nobody swims. In fact April averages about 23°C with the garúa fog lifting, sea around 20°C, negligible rain and roughly 185 hours of sunshine. We also said Peru has no resort, and that is only true of Lima itself. About 240 km and three hours south is Paracas, a genuine resort bay: Hotel Paracas with 120 rooms, two heated pools and its own pier, alongside La Hacienda Bahía Paracas and The Legend. Boats leave the hotel dock for the Ballestas Islands, the Paracas National Reserve is next door, and Nazca overflights go from nearby. As resort-plus-real-places goes, that is a genuinely good answer, and Lima on the way through is one of the best food cities in the world.

What stops it is the journey rather than the destination. There is no nonstop from Guadalajara to Lima; every routing connects, usually through Mexico City or Panama, for roughly 9h43 of travel, and then Paracas adds a three-hour drive on the far side. That is effectively a two-day journey in each direction, and easily the hardest arrival on this entire list for everybody, Patricia most of all. Machu Picchu, which is what most people actually mean when they say Peru, compounds it: a separate flight to Cusco and 11,000 feet of altitude. The honest summary is that this would be a wonderful trip for a subset of this family in a different year. It is the wrong trip for this particular birthday. Patricia, we’d still like to know what drew you to it — if it was the food and a real city rather than a beach, that points somewhere else entirely, maybe Mexico City or Mérida, and we should talk about that.

06Cartagena, ColombiaNo

Nobody proposed this one; we added it because it answers the brief unusually well on paper. The walled colonial city is a real landmark rather than a manufactured excursion, the Rosario Islands supply the beach half an hour offshore, and it would be new to everyone, which counts for something on a milestone. Copa makes it easy from Panama and KLM flies it year-round from Amsterdam, so Karen and Pancho and Gloria are both well covered.

Then it fails the same test Aruba failed. There is no nonstop from Boston or Chicago, so Ben and Christy both connect through Miami, New York, Fort Lauderdale or Atlanta, and Guadalajara needs connections too. Given that the entire argument for Cancún is that nobody connects, importing connections for three of the six parties in exchange for novelty is a bad trade. It is listed so you can see it was considered and turned down for a reason, not overlooked.

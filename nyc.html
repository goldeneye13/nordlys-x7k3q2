new
Masquerade — immersive Phantom GOING FAST
Not a Broadway show — a walk-through. Andrew Lloyd Webber’s Phantom score, but rebuilt by director Diane Paulus as the Phantom’s own masquerade ball. You move through several floors of 218 W 57th — rehearsal rooms, dressing rooms, the lair, a rooftop, a ballroom — guided in a group, with actors pulling people in: the Phantom may take your hand, you might be handed a letter to read. It adds material the stage show never had (his childhood in a freak show, how he got the mask) and keeps the ones you know, chandelier and Music of the Night included. Six staggered start times a night, 15 minutes apart, each with a different Phantom and Christine, so no two groups see quite the same cast. Candlelit bar afterwards. From $240, and Dad says they are moving.
The fine print he left out: ~2 hours, no interval, on your feet throughout, with several flights of stairs and some escalators. Dress code is cocktail or formal in black, white or silver only — cloaks lent at the door if you turn up wrong. Mask required, provided free. No thin heels, there is a grate walkway. No physical ticket: a password lands 24 hours ahead, shown with photo ID. Age 13+.
masqueradenyc.comDress code & FAQ
⚑
Getting around inside — arrange it when you book CHECKED
It is a walking show with stairs and no seats to go back to, so the options are worth knowing up front rather than at the door. There is an elevator, and the venue will lend a wheelchair with a staff member accompanying you for the whole show to anyone who would rather not be on their feet for two hours — and they walk you to a clear view at each scene, so you miss nothing. All of it has to be requested in advance. Worth one email to the venue before we book for a group.

💵 The numbers are dinner, per person, food only — before drinks, tax and tip, which in New York realistically add 40–50%. Lunch runs well under these at almost every one. Exact figures (River Café, SAGA, Yingtao, EMP) are the restaurants’ own published menu prices as of Sep 2026; the ~ ones are estimates. Hell’s Kitchen — the pre-theatre neighbourhood

🎭 Why this block matters for this trip: Chicago (Ambassador, W 49th), Hamilton (Richard Rodgers, W 46th) and Masquerade (218 W 57th) all sit inside a few blocks of here. The Ninth Avenue corridor eats better than the Times Square core at the same money — the trade is an 8–12 minute walk to the theatre instead of 3–5. Book early seatings; the whole neighbourhood turns over at 7:30.
W46
Becco ~$50
355 W 46th on Restaurant Row, Lidia Bastianich, since 1992. The Sinfonia di Paste is the draw: an antipasto and then unlimited servings of three pastas the waiters bring round, about $37, with a $40-a-bottle wine list. It takes large parties and it is built for a curtain time — the strongest pre-theatre option here for a group.
becco-nyc.com
W46
Joe Allen ~$60
326 W 46th, since 1965, and the room the theatre world actually eats in. Burgers, meatloaf, strong martinis, and a wall hung entirely with posters from flops — the bigger the disaster the more prized the poster. Worth going for the wall alone.
joeallenrestaurant.com
9th
Marseille ~$70
630 Ninth Av at 44th. The neighbourhood’s French brasserie for about twenty years — oysters, bouillabaisse, escargot, steak frites. Big room, sidewalk tables for watching Ninth Avenue, and it handles a group before or after a show.
marseillenyc.com

Museums, with what’s actually up during Oct 14–21, 2026. Left column is travel time from the hotel.

🗓 Closure days matter more than usual here. The Met closes Wednesdays. The Guggenheim has been closing Thursdays. The Whitney, the Morgan and the Brooklyn Museum close Mondays — which is exactly the day Dad set aside for museums. Confirm each one’s calendar the week before.

Krasner and Pollock: Past Continuous (opens Oct 4) is the headline — the two of them traced side by side. Also up: Orientalism: Between Fact and Fantasy, A King’s Carpet: Louis XIV and the Savonnerie, Chasing Clouds, Si Lewen’s Parade, and Liu Wei on the facade. Closed Wednesdays.
